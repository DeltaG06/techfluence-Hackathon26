FATF RED FLAGS & INTERNAL FRAUD DETECTION GUIDELINES
Based on Financial Action Task Force (FATF) Guidance
Adapted for Internal Audit Use — Version 1.4

1. TRANSACTION STRUCTURING RED FLAGS
   - Multiple transactions between $8,000 – $9,999 within any 48-hour window from
     the same account or related accounts (classic structuring / smurfing pattern).
   - Repeated payments to the same payee in amounts just below approval thresholds
     (e.g., multiple $4,900 payments instead of a single $9,800 payment).
   - Sequential transactions in identical or near-identical amounts within minutes of
     each other from the same account.
   - Payments in suspiciously round amounts (exactly $5,000.00, $10,000.00, etc.)
     without an invoice or business justification.

2. ACCOUNT TAKEOVER & INSIDER FRAUD INDICATORS
   - A single employee account responsible for a disproportionately high volume of
     high-value transactions compared to peers in the same department.
   - Transactions initiated by an employee in a department inconsistent with their
     role (e.g., an HR employee initiating Finance TRANSFER transactions).
   - Transactions approved by the same person who initiated them (dual control failure).
   - Multiple failed authentication attempts followed immediately by a large transaction.
   - Transactions processed during an employee's known leave or vacation period.

3. VENDOR FRAUD RED FLAGS
   - Payments to vendors that have never received a payment before (no transaction history).
   - Vendor bank account details changed within 30 days prior to a large payment.
   - Multiple payments to different vendors sharing the same bank account number.
   - Payments to vendors whose name or tax ID does not match the approved vendor register.
   - Vendor transaction count below 3 combined with payment amount above department average
     is a high-risk combination requiring immediate review.

4. CASH-OUT & TRANSFER ABUSE PATTERNS
   - CASH_OUT transactions to customer accounts (accounts starting with "C") are a
     primary PaySim fraud signal. These transactions should always be reviewed when
     the balance drain exceeds 80%.
   - TRANSFER transactions exceeding $1,000,000 to a customer (non-merchant) account
     require escalation to the Chief Compliance Officer (CCO) immediately.
   - Any CASH_OUT or TRANSFER where the original account balance is fully drained
     (oldBalance – newBalance = originalBalance, i.e., 100% drain) is a critical fraud
     indicator and must be blocked pending investigation.
   - DEBIT transactions processed on weekends or public holidays with amounts exceeding
     the 99th percentile for that department are flagged for review.

5. VELOCITY & BEHAVIORAL ANOMALY INDICATORS
   - Transaction amount more than 3x the department's 90-day rolling average is a
     significant anomaly ("amount_vs_dept_avg" ratio > 3.0 triggers HIGH classification).
   - An account's transaction frequency increasing by more than 200% in a 7-day period
     compared to the prior 30-day average indicates potential account compromise.
   - High anomaly scores (above 0.75 on normalized isolation forest score) combined
     with destination account type being a customer account requires human review.
   - Transactions in the top 1% of all amounts (above the 99th percentile) should
     always be reviewed regardless of other risk factors.

6. GEOGRAPHIC & TIMING RISK INDICATORS
   - Transactions initiated between 23:00 and 05:00 local time carry elevated risk.
   - Weekend transactions (is_weekend = 1) with amounts above $10,000 must be reviewed.
   - Transactions to or from accounts in multiple jurisdictions within 24 hours
     without a documented business reason constitute a FATF geographic risk indicator.
