AML TRANSACTION MONITORING POLICY
Based on Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) and FinCEN Requirements
Version 3.2 | Internal Compliance Document

1. CURRENCY TRANSACTION REPORTING (CTR)
   - Any single cash transaction or aggregated cash transactions in a single business day
     exceeding $10,000 must be reported via a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) to FinCEN.
   - Aggregation applies to all transactions by or on behalf of the same person in the
     same business day, even across multiple accounts or branches.
   - Structuring transactions to avoid the $10,000 CTR threshold is a federal crime under
     31 U.S.C. § 5324, regardless of the source of the funds.

2. SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY REPORTS (SAR)
   - A SAR must be filed for any transaction of $5,000 or more where the institution
     knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect that the transaction:
       (a) Involves funds derived from illegal activity.
       (b) Is designed to evade reporting requirements (structuring).
       (c) Has no lawful purpose or is not the type of transaction the customer
           would normally be expected to engage in.
   - A SAR must be filed for any transaction of $25,000 or more if suspicious,
     regardless of whether a suspect can be identified.
   - Filing deadline: SAR must be submitted no later than 30 calendar days after the
     date of initial detection. If no suspect is identified, the deadline may be
     extended to 60 days.
   - Confidentiality: Staff must never disclose to any person that a SAR has been
     filed. Tipping off a subject is a federal violation.

3. HIGH-RISK TRANSACTION PATTERNS TO MONITOR
   - STRUCTURING (Smurfing): Multiple transactions just below $10,000 threshold.
     Flag any series of transactions between $8,000 and $9,999 within 48 hours
     from the same account or related accounts.
   - RAPID FUND MOVEMENT: Funds deposited and then immediately transferred out
     within 24 hours with no clear economic purpose.
   - UNUSUAL VELOCITY: Account transaction volume or frequency increases by more
     than 300% above the 90-day rolling average without business justification.
   - ROUND NUMBER TRANSACTIONS: Frequent transactions in round dollar amounts
     (e.g., exactly $5,000, $10,000, $50,000) are a known money laundering indicator.
   - BALANCE DRAIN: Any transaction that drains more than 90% of an account balance
     in a single transfer must be escalated to the compliance team immediately.

4. CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE (CDD) TRIGGERS
   - Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) is required for:
       * Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs).
       * Customers from FATF high-risk jurisdictions.
       * Any customer whose transaction patterns deviate significantly from their
         declared business purpose or monthly transaction profile.
   - Account activity inconsistent with stated occupation or business type requires
     immediate review.
