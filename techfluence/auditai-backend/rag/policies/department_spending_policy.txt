DEPARTMENT-LEVEL SPENDING CONTROLS & EXPENSE POLICY
Internal Finance Policy — All Departments
Version 4.0 | Last Reviewed: Q1 2024

1. DEPARTMENTAL TRANSACTION LIMITS & BASELINES
   MARKETING (PAYMENT category):
   - Typical transaction range: $100 – $15,000 per payment.
   - Single transactions above $20,000 require VP Marketing approval.
   - Payments to new media vendors require prior campaign brief sign-off.
   - Aggregate monthly spend above $500,000 triggers a finance committee review.

   FINANCE (TRANSFER category):
   - Typical transaction range: $1,000 – $200,000 per transfer.
   - TRANSFER transactions above $500,000 require CFO approval.
   - TRANSFER transactions above $1,000,000 require CFO + Board notification.
   - All Finance-initiated TRANSFER transactions must have a corresponding
     purchase order (PO) or approved budget line item.
   - Transfers to accounts outside the approved counterparty list require
     enhanced due diligence (EDD) before processing.

   OPERATIONS (CASH_OUT category):
   - Typical transaction range: $500 – $50,000.
   - CASH_OUT transactions above $100,000 require COO and CFO dual approval.
   - CASH_OUT to customer accounts (non-merchant) is prohibited without explicit
     compliance approval and documented business justification.
   - All CASH_OUT transactions must be reconciled against operational invoices
     within 5 business days.

   HR (DEBIT category):
   - Covers payroll, contractor payments, and benefits disbursements.
   - Individual DEBIT transactions above $50,000 require HR Director + CFO approval.
   - Payroll runs outside the scheduled payroll calendar (1st and 15th of each month)
     require HR Director authorization and Finance sign-off.
   - DEBIT transactions to non-employee personal accounts require Finance Director
     approval and must be documented as approved advances or settlements.

   ENGINEERING (CASH_IN category):
   - Covers software licenses, SaaS subscriptions, and infrastructure payments.
   - Individual transactions above $25,000 require Engineering VP approval.
   - Annual subscription renewals above $100,000 require IT committee review.
   - Emergency infrastructure purchases above $10,000 require CTO notification.

2. GENERAL EXPENSE CONTROLS (ALL DEPARTMENTS)
   - Segregation of Duties: The employee who requests a payment must never be
     the same employee who approves or processes it.
   - Receipt Requirements: All transactions above $50 require documented proof of
     purchase. Transactions above $500 require itemized receipts.
   - Submission Deadline: Expense reports must be submitted within 30 days of
     the transaction date. Late submissions require manager justification.
   - No personal purchases may be made using corporate accounts or payment methods.
   - Split transactions (deliberately breaking one purchase into multiple smaller
     transactions to bypass approval thresholds) are prohibited and constitute fraud.

3. AUDIT TRAIL REQUIREMENTS
   - All transactions must be logged with: initiating employee ID, approver ID,
     timestamp, purpose/category, vendor ID, and final amount.
   - Transaction logs are immutable. Any attempt to modify or delete a log entry
     is a serious compliance violation requiring immediate escalation to the CCO.
   - Monthly reconciliation reports must be reviewed by an independent Finance
     team member not involved in the original transaction processing.
   - Exception reports (flagged transactions) must be resolved within 10 business days.
     Unresolved exceptions escalate automatically to department VP.

4. ANTI-FRAUD TRAINING & AWARENESS
   - All employees handling financial transactions must complete annual AML and
     fraud awareness training (minimum 4 hours per year).
   - Employees must immediately report any transaction they believe to be suspicious
     to the compliance hotline — failure to report is itself a policy violation.
   - Business Email Compromise (BEC) awareness: Never process payment instructions
     received solely via email without independent phone verification, regardless
     of how urgent the request appears or how senior the sender claims to be.
